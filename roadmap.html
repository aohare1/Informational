Game Dev Roadmap

A step-by-step path from raw idea to finished, released game — no experience required to start.

The Full Roadmap Overview

Game development follows a consistent pipeline, whether you're a solo indie dev or part of a large studio.

  1. Concept & Idea
  2. Pre-production & Planning
  3. Prototype
  4. Core Loop Development
  5. Content Production
  6. Alpha Testing
  7. Beta & Polish
  8. Launch & Post-launch

Stage 1 — Concept & Idea

Every game starts with an idea. At this stage, define your genre, core hook, target audience, and platforms. Keep your first project small — a simple finished game beats a complex abandoned one.

  • What genre is your game?
  • What makes it unique?
  • Who is your target audience?
  • What platforms will you target?

Stage 2 — Pre-Production

Before building anything, plan it out.

  • Game Design Document (GDD)
  • Art style references and mood boards
  • Choosing your engine and tools
  • Scope definition — what's in and what's out

Stage 3 — Prototype

A prototype is a rough, fast version of your core mechanic. Don't worry about art or polish — just prove the idea is fun.

  • Build in 1–2 weeks max for small projects
  • Use placeholder art (boxes, circles)
  • Test with real people early

Stage 4 — Core Loop

The core loop is the repeating cycle that keeps players engaged: play → challenge → reward → repeat.

  • Implement main gameplay systems
  • Add basic progression and rewards
  • Set up save/load systems

Stage 5 — Content Production

This is often the longest phase — filling your game with levels, enemies, items, dialogue, and everything the player experiences.

  • Create levels and environments
  • Add enemies, NPCs, and items
  • Write dialogue and story content
  • Produce final art assets and audio

Stage 6 — Alpha Testing

Alpha is when the game is feature-complete but rough. Share it with trusted testers to find bugs and gameplay issues.

  • Closed testing with friends or community
  • Bug tracking and fixing
  • Gameplay balance adjustments

Stage 7 — Beta & Polish

Polish turns a good game into a great one. No new mechanics — only refining what exists.

  • Screen shake, particle effects, sound design
  • Performance optimization
  • Accessibility options
  • UI/UX refinement

Stage 8 — Launch & Post-Launch

Plan your launch carefully — marketing and community building matter just as much as the game itself.

  • Set up store pages (Steam, itch.io, App Store)
  • Plan a launch trailer and marketing push
  • Monitor player feedback post-launch
  • Patch bugs and release updates
